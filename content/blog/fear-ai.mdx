## The Great AI Panic

Arguments about AI and jobs often move directly from a model demonstration to a prediction about an industry. If the system can perform the task, the job is assumed to be next. That skips much of the work required to put a new technology into use.

The organization has to decide who can rely on the result, who is responsible when it fails, and which existing process will change. I think those decisions explain part of the gap between rapid model progress and slower changes in how work gets done.

## The Two-Speed Economy

Some workflows are easy to experiment with. A small software team can try an assistant on a draft, inspect the result, and discard it with little coordination. The person who benefits may also be the person choosing the tool and reviewing the work.

Other workflows distribute those roles across an institution. The user wants less administrative work, procurement controls the budget, another team owns the records, and an approval process determines what can change. A better model does not merge those responsibilities.

The difference can exist inside the same company. Drafting an internal note and updating a customer's official account record may use similar language capabilities while carrying very different consequences. Adoption speed depends on the workflow receiving the technology.

That makes organizational change part of the product problem. If the person who saves time cannot approve the purchase, or the team assuming the risk receives none of the benefit, the vendor needs a plan for that mismatch. Another benchmark improvement may leave it untouched.

## The Hard Part Is Getting It Used

A better model can make a task technically possible while leaving the reasons nobody adopts it untouched. The buyer, the budget, the existing workflow, and the rules around changing it still determine whether the product gets used.

That is where I think some of the difficult AI work lies. The team has to understand the system that receives the technology, including who benefits from changing it and who has a reason to keep it as it is.
