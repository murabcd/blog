## Staying in the Same Place

An outbound team adopts AI to send more messages. Buyers respond by using stronger filters. The seller increases volume again, and the inbox becomes harder for everyone to use. Each side improves its own process while the overall interaction gets more expensive.

This resembles the Red Queen effect: an advantage prompts a response that erodes it. AI can improve individual tasks while also changing the environment in which those tasks compete.

## Where the System Becomes a Contest

Hiring gives another example. A company can automate parts of screening, while a candidate uses tools such as [Cluely](https://cluely.com/) during an interview. If the assessment depends on unaided live performance, the company then has to decide how to verify the signal or redesign the exercise.

Sales tools such as [AI SDRs](https://aisdr.com/) can reduce the effort needed to generate outreach. They cannot ensure that the recipient has a reason to respond. As volume grows, attention and credibility can become the constraints.

These mechanisms are enough to examine without assuming every company experiences the same result. Measure the downstream decision before concluding that higher activity improved the business.

## Count the Work on Both Sides

An automated message has a low production cost, but someone else may have to filter, verify, or answer it. A generated application can be cheap for the applicant and expensive for the reviewer. Local efficiency can shift effort to the other participant.

A product team should ask where that effort goes. Track whether the interaction produces a useful match or completed task, and what the recipient must do to get there. Output volume alone can reward the behavior that makes the shared channel worse.

## Trust Becomes Scarce

As automated output becomes abundant, people need better reasons to give something their attention. A recommendation from someone who understands the problem can carry more weight than another generated application. A conversation can resolve uncertainty that a polished support answer leaves untouched.

That is why I think human trust becomes more valuable as producing plausible material gets cheaper. The person on the receiving end still has to decide what is worth believing, buying, or acting on. More output gives them more of that work.
