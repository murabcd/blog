## The Challenge in Specialized Work

A contract assistant can produce fluent language while missing a term that changes the agreement's meaning. The professional reviewing it needs to know which source informed the suggestion, which policy applies, and what still requires judgment.

Based on my experience building AI features within a contract lifecycle management platform, I would treat those requirements as part of the product architecture. The assistant needs access to the relevant material and a bounded role in the review process.

## Rules with a Clear Scope

Legal teams may define preferred terms, liability limits, or jurisdictional requirements for a contract type. Store those rules with the templates or policies they apply to, and make the relationship visible in the interface.

Natural-language instructions can express intent, but the system still needs a way to check the result. Where a rule can be validated deterministically, use that check. Route uncertain interpretations to a qualified reviewer rather than treating a fluent explanation as compliance.

## Privacy and Data Handling

Contractual data can be sensitive. Establish where it is processed, which services receive it, who may access it, how long it is retained, and what the provider's data-use terms permit. These choices need to fit the organization's requirements.

Keep permissions consistent with the existing platform. An assistant should not expose a clause or agreement that the person asking could not otherwise access. Record enough of the operation to investigate errors without unnecessarily duplicating sensitive material.

## Fit the Contract Workflow

Place assistance where drafting, review, and analysis already happen. Preserve the link between a suggestion, the source agreement, and the current version so the reviewer can inspect the change in context.

Some tasks may fit a separate interface, but the result still has to return to the authoritative workflow. Extra copying and reconciliation can consume the time the assistant was intended to save.

## Tradeoffs to Evaluate

Model choice, retrieval, and domain-specific evaluation all affect quality and cost. Test representative clauses and failure cases before deciding that fine-tuning or a larger model is the missing piece.

Latency also matters in interactive review. A slower, more complete analysis may be appropriate for a full agreement while a drafting suggestion needs a shorter response. Define the task and review obligation before optimizing either.

## Keep the Reviewer in Control

A useful assistant makes the proposed change easier to assess. It shows the source, relevant policy, and unresolved uncertainty while leaving a clear path to accept, edit, or reject the suggestion.

Evaluate the product on the quality of that completed review, including the time needed to catch mistakes. Generating more clauses is a weak measure if professionals have to reconstruct every recommendation before using it.
