## The Gap between a Demo and Delegation

AGI timelines are difficult to judge from product work alone. A model can solve a difficult isolated problem and still struggle to complete a business task across changing systems. My skepticism about near-term forecasts comes from that second kind of work.

This is a view from building with the technology, not a research claim about what is theoretically possible. I want to see whether a system can carry feedback forward, recover from tool failures, and complete a task whose quality takes more than a test result to assess.

## Carrying a Correction Forward

In tasks such as organizing customer feedback or drafting release notes, a model can produce a useful first version and still need the same corrections repeatedly. The immediate conversation improves while a later session starts without the relevant lesson.

Stored instructions, examples, retrieval, and fine-tuning can address different parts of this problem. They also create work: deciding what to retain, preventing a narrow correction from becoming a universal rule, and checking behavior after an update.

The practical requirement is continuity. I need the next relevant task to benefit from the correction without unrelated tasks inheriting a bad assumption.

## What Onboarding Actually Teaches

A new engineer learns conventions through code review, examples, questions, and the consequences of their changes. Some lessons can be written down; others only become clear after several tasks reveal the pattern.

An agent system needs an explicit mechanism for preserving that feedback. A summary can help, but it may omit the condition that made a rule appropriate. The test is whether the system later applies the lesson in the right place and ignores it where it does not belong.

That is a concrete evaluation problem. A larger context window or another instruction can be useful, but neither establishes that the learning loop works.

## Reliable Work Across Applications

Consider a quarterly business review. An agent might need sales data, engagement metrics, support trends, and financial figures, then reconcile them and build a presentation. It also needs to notice when two systems use different definitions of the same metric.

Several challenges appear before slide generation:

1. **Access and interpretation:** The system must obtain the right data and understand its scope.
2. **Long feedback loops:** A person may need to inspect substantial work before identifying the first wrong assumption.
3. **Recovery:** A failed export, changed permission, or incomplete record must not silently become a confident claim.

These difficulties make me cautious about treating a good demonstration as evidence that the whole job can be delegated. Measure the completed task under the conditions in which someone would actually rely on it.

## Keep the Progress and the Limits Visible

The models available when I formed this view could reason through difficult prompts and generate useful software. That progress is part of why the remaining problems are worth examining carefully. Better isolated capability creates more opportunities to test the surrounding system.

I would update my expectations when agents consistently complete these longer tasks with bounded review and predictable recovery. Until then, the most useful questions are about the failures we can reproduce and the mechanisms that would remove them, rather than confidence in a date.
